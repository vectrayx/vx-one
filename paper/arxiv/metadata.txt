title: VX-One: Typed, Calibrated Decision Heads for Real-Time Perimeter Triage - An empirical study of unknown-attack honesty, cascade gray zones, and calibration under drift
authors: Juan S. Santillana
abstract: Perimeter triage faces a latency/semantics trade-off: signature engines are fast and format-safe but blind to unseen attacks, while generative models are semantic but slow and produce unparseable output. In late 2026 a class of commercial System One decision models (TypeSafe Jev; Microsoft-Decision-1) began selling typed decision primitives - binary gates, closed-set choices, ordinal scores - as product APIs, with no academic treatment or independent evaluation. We present VX-One: a small multi-head network (100K--10M parameters) whose outputs are these primitives—a calibrated gate, a choice over a closed candidate set that always includes an explicit unknown option, and an ordinal score (conditional ordinal regression), trained multi-task, temperature-calibrated per head (~0.7--1.7 ms single-event latency p50 on CPU). We evaluate it on three public benchmarks (UNSW-NB15, CIC-IDS2017, EMBER2024) against calibrated XGBoost, Random-Forest, MLP, and Kitsune baselines, and measure a two-phase cascade (Suricata with 52{,674 Emerging-Threats rules -> VX-One) over five days of CIC-IDS2017. Our findings are deliberately double-sided: (i) VX-One wins detection (AUROC/FPR@95%TPR) against every calibrated baseline on UNSW-NB15 and under temporal drift on CIC-IDS2017 (0.711 vs.\ 0.637), but calibrated XGBoost wins multi-class attribution on all three benchmarks; (ii) under leave-one-family-out the gate detects never-seen families on 9/9 UNSW folds yet drops below chance on CIC's recon family, and an abstention threshold calibrated on one benchmark transfers poorly to another; (iii) the signature tier's gray zone is real, not a reconstruction artifact: Suricata with the full ET Open ruleset resolves ~0.1% of daily attacks on flow reconstructions and only 0.2--2.2% on the original payload-bearing PCAPs (0.45% DDoS, 0.06% PortScan on the wave day); an in-distribution Phase 2 recovers the zone (combined recall >=0.977), while a strictly by-day Phase 2 recovers only the DoS/DDoS share (0.34 recall, 0.97 precision on the wave day); (iv) probability calibration collapses under temporal drift (ECE 0.04->0.40) for every method while ranking partially survives. We contribute not novel estimators but a measured account of where typed calibrated decisions help perimeter triage, and where they fail.
